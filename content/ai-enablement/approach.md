---
title: "How I approach AI enablement"
description: "A practical operating sequence for selecting, testing, governing, and improving AI-assisted workflows."
---

AI enablement begins with a task, its users, and an owner. A model is one component of the solution, not the operating system.

## Diagnose the actual workflow

Identify who does the work, what information they need, where handoffs fail, and which steps require judgment. Do not assume automation is the answer before diagnosing the friction.

## Bound one useful use case

Specify the user group, approved sources, input and output expectations, permissions, human decision rights, and stop conditions. Assess value alongside implementation effort and risk.

## Build with the people doing the work

Prototype a small change in an existing content or knowledge workflow. Examples include research-to-draft production, structured source retrieval, reusable technical briefs, and governed review paths.

## Validate before widening access

Test representative tasks against source fidelity, factual accuracy, escalation behavior, reviewer effort, and failure cases. Record what worked, what did not, and what still needs human intervention.

## Pilot, measure, and improve

Start with a responsible owner and baseline. Useful measures can include correction rates, review time, content cycle time, reuse of trusted sources, and task completion. Expand only when the workflow earns its place.

## The operating rule

Build first. Learn from real use. Standardize what works. Stop or revise what does not.

This is an approach and an engagement model, not a claim that every proposed workflow has been deployed or produced independently measured results.
