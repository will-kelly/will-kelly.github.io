# AI enablement that gets built

AI enablement is not a tool rollout, a prompt library, or a strategy deck that leaves everyone wondering what to do on Monday.

I help content, marketing, knowledge, and technical teams put AI to work in the systems they already use. The aim is straightforward: identify the work worth changing, build practical workflows with the people who will own them, and establish the judgment, governance, and operating habits that keep the work useful after the pilot ends.

## Build first. Learn in the work.

The quickest way to make AI feel abstract is to study it from a distance. I work embedded with the people closest to the work: writers, editors, subject-matter experts, product marketers, solution architects, sales teams, and the operators responsible for the knowledge behind the output.

Together, we map the friction in a real workflow, make a small but meaningful intervention, and evaluate what changes. That might mean redesigning a research-to-draft process, structuring a reusable knowledge base, turning scattered field insight into governed source material, or improving the handoffs that slow down technical content and GTM work.

The goal is not just a recommendation. It is a working example, a repeatable playbook, and a team that has learned how to improve the next workflow without waiting for another consultant.

## Where I help

### Find the work AI should actually change

Not every process needs AI. I assess workflows for repetitive effort, costly context switching, uneven quality, slow review cycles, inaccessible expertise, and knowledge that is trapped in documents, inboxes, or individual heads. Then I help prioritize use cases against business value, implementation effort, risk, and the team’s ability to sustain them.

### Design workflows people will use

A strong AI workflow makes human judgment more valuable, not less visible. I work with teams to define the inputs, source material, review points, escalation paths, and acceptable outputs before they automate or scale anything. The result is a workflow that fits real deadlines, team habits, and existing platforms, rather than one that collapses outside a demo.

### Make knowledge usable and trustworthy

AI quality rises or falls on the quality, structure, permissions, and currency of the information behind it. I help teams turn sprawling knowledge systems into usable operating assets: clearer information architecture, source-of-truth decisions, metadata and templates, ownership, review cadences, and practical rules for what AI may retrieve, summarize, transform, or leave alone.

### Establish sensible governance

Governance should make good work easier, not create a committee around every prompt. I help set proportionate guardrails for accuracy, confidentiality, intellectual property, attribution, brand voice, accessibility, and human review. The goal is enough structure to prevent predictable mistakes and enough freedom for teams to learn.

### Drive adoption through participation

Most AI adoption fails because a tool is handed down to people who were never shown how it helps their specific work. I build with the people who will use the workflows, create examples from their real materials, and equip internal champions with templates, decision rules, and training that respects different skill levels. Adoption becomes a capability, not a compliance exercise.

### Measure whether it is earning its place

More generated content is not a success metric. I help teams define practical measures such as time-to-first-draft, reviewer burden, reuse of trusted knowledge, correction rates, content cycle time, SME availability, and downstream business outcomes. Those measures make it easier to improve the system and stop funding activity that only looks innovative.

My approach connects AI enablement to the operational layer where it succeeds or fails. I bring a writer’s respect for audience, accuracy, and voice; a product marketer’s focus on customer and business value; and hands-on experience helping teams adopt documentation and knowledge practices across tools such as Notion, Confluence, and SharePoint.

I also understand technical work. Years spent writing about cloud, DevOps, DevSecOps, Kubernetes, enterprise software, and AI mean I can work comfortably with engineers and technical subject-matter experts without turning their expertise into generic marketing language. I can translate between the people building a system, the people explaining it, and the people deciding whether it is worth the investment.

That combination matters because AI enablement is rarely one team’s problem. It crosses content, product, sales, customer success, IT, security, legal, and leadership. Someone has to make the handoffs visible and turn good intentions into a shared way of working.
