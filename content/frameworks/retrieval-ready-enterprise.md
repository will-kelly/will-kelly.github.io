---
title: "Retrieval-Ready Enterprise"
description: "A knowledge operations model for reliable, governed use by employees and AI systems."
---

A better chatbot cannot repair a knowledge base full of outdated, conflicting, or unowned material. **Retrieval-Ready Enterprise** treats trusted organizational knowledge as infrastructure.

## What makes knowledge retrieval-ready?

An individual item should make its **claim, evidence, authority, audience, owner, status, review date, trust level, permissions, and sensitivity** clear where those attributes apply.

The lifecycle is **Create → Capture → Structure → Validate → Retrieve → Apply → Evolve → Retire**.

## Assess five areas

- **Authority and trust:** Is the answer attributable to an approved, relevant source?
- **Structure and findability:** Can people and systems retrieve the right item in context?
- **Ownership and freshness:** Who maintains it, and when does it expire?
- **Access and governance:** Do permissions and sensitivity rules survive retrieval?
- **Use and feedback:** Can users challenge an answer, correct it, and improve the source?

## First implementation

Pick one high-value domain, inventory its documents and access rules, identify the authoritative versions, add ownership and review metadata, and test retrieval against real questions. Include contradictory-source and unauthorized-access test cases.

## What to measure

Retrieval relevance, unsupported-answer frequency, stale-answer incidence, source coverage, correction turnaround, and owner workload.

**Status:** Knowledge readiness and governance model. Reliability must be tested in the target environment.
